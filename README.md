# micr.dev/about
[screenshot]

I have always struggled with really bad pasive memory recall, so not only I decided to make an About Me webpage to help people know me a bit better, but funnily enough, so I wouldnt forget things that interest me.

The page is inspired by some of the art made for the album "Syro" by Aphex Twin, as clearly noticeable once compared side by side. For some reason, I fell in love with the minimalism and simplicity of it, and decided to take heavily inspiration for my page.

[screenshot of syro]

It starts with a small description of myself and the page, aswell as my prefered pronouns. Its then followed by things and stuff that interest me, in the following order: Series, Animes, Films, Games, Styles, Runways, Fashion Designers, Fragances, Colours, Fonts, Bands, Indie Artists, EPs / Albums, Individual Tracks, Genres, Inspirations & References, and then a small section with completely random short ones. Some of these sections use images to showcase the items if I felt like it matched better, and others allow you to click them as to open that certain item in a new page (like a specific song). Hovering over the images displays a purposefully made simple and sharp black rectangle, with the name of what youre hovering over inside of it, in case you didnt recognize it from the picture itself and wanted to know what it was.

The art replacing the Syro CD is made by @masarapmaruya. Its a full body commission of my character Quarzite.

All of the data is cleanly structured in a .json file, allowing me to add or remove anything whenever I want. The font used, just like in the original cover art, is DecimaMono.

# micr.dev/quarzite
[screenshot]

I created my character Quarzite back in 2022 as a some sort of way to display my online persona. Throughout the years, I slowly but surely been collecting commissions and other stuff from artists, but never really had anywhere to showcase them. I commissioned them for my own personal enjoyment, and to use as profile pictures and such in different platforms. That changed when I discovered Dimden's OC Gallery (https://dimden.dev/gallery/) at the start of 2025. Since then, I had always wanted to make my own gallery for my own character; and its in part one of the reasons I decided to make my whole website in the first place (not only the Quarzite section, but this whole repository in itself).

I wanted to make something simple, as to not distract from the character itself, but still cool to see and interact with. Not stealing the attention but without boring the user. I thought about making a Neocities Y2K style website, but quickly got demotivated seeing what people managed to do and knowing I would never achieve something near it without sinking a lot of hours into it. Even months. They say comparison is the thief of joy, and that was partly true. The itch stayed in my brain until I discovered 98.css (https://jdan.github.io/98.css), and thats when I knew I had to stop making up excuses and get to work.

I wanted the "Gallery" to give the vibes of an anime image posting forum, so I made 200x200 containers and let the images automatically adapt to it. If you wanted to take a closer look in higher quality, you can click on them and they will open a popup window with some more info about them, like the date I commissioned them, the artist, and sometimes a small description if I feel like it fits. The "Information" window also gave me a excuse to think more deeply about my character, having to describe them physically and personality wise. Last but not least, I was missing a window in order to not make it asymmetrical (the bad type of asymmetrical, not the good one). It started as a simple notepad window where people could type stuff, which then developed in different font sizes and randomly chosen messages written on them. With the recent (as of me typing this) resurgence of Clippy, I also used it as an excuse to add something that I didnt feel like it fit in the About Me webpage: quotes or phrases that I like (it looked kinda corny / out of place). It was a bit of a challenge, but I managed to make it look good and work; aswell as allowing it to overflow when a specific message is said. I wanted to add something more, and thats when I discovered jspaint by 1j01, which allowed me to easily add another random element that could popup. After all of this was finished, I had 28 total random scenarios (22 Clippy Quotes, 5 Notepad Versions and 1 Paint Window, although this last one was upped to have 3/30 chance of appearing instead of 1/30.).

All of this was topped with different original Windows 98 changeable tilesets, sound effects, and draggable & resizable (most of them) windows. Oh, and dont forget the good ol' Neko cursor cat.
